Getting licensed in Massachusetts

Step by step, from zero to legal. Fees and license sales are all on MassFishHunt. Licenses run on the calendar year and expire Dec 31.

Hunting

Covers bow and firearm hunting. Everyone new to hunting starts here.

  1. Take the online hunter ed courseWork through MassWildlife's online Basic Hunter Education study material. It's free and self-paced, and you need it done before you can sign up for a field day.
  2. Register for and attend a field dayA free, in-person day with instructors covering safe firearm handling, ethics, and laws, ending in a written test. Seats fill fast, so watch the course list and use waitlists.
  3. Get your certificatePass the test and you're certified for life. Keep a copy; you'll need it to buy your first license and for an FID.
  4. Buy a Hunting or Sporting licenseSporting covers hunting and freshwater fishing together. Resident licenses include 2 antlered deer tags good statewide.
  5. Add stamps and permits for what you'll huntArchery Stamp for the archery deer season. Primitive Firearms Stamp for the muzzleloader season. Waterfowl also needs a state waterfowl stamp, a federal duck stamp, and HIP registration. Antlerless deer permits are applied for in summer on MassFishHunt.
  6. In the field: tag, report, wear orangeTag deer, turkey, and bear right away and report the harvest online. During shotgun and primitive seasons, wear 500 square inches of blaze orange on chest, back, and head.
Bowhunting needs no extra course in Massachusetts: hunter ed, a license, and the Archery Stamp.

FID card (rifles and shotguns)

A hunting license does not let you own or carry a gun. To possess a rifle or shotgun you also need a Firearms Identification Card.

  1. Get a qualifying safety certificateA Massachusetts Basic Hunter Education certificate counts for an FID. So does a state-approved Basic Firearms Safety course. Hunter ed from another state does not count here.
  2. Know the 2026 rulesSince April 2, 2026, safety courses must cover MA firearms law, storage, and suicide prevention, and the law calls for live fire. Live fire isn't enforced yet while regulations are finalized. Hunter ed is not accepted for a License to Carry, only an FID.
  3. Apply through your local police departmentContact your town's firearms licensing office for the application, fee, and an appointment. Expect fingerprinting, a background check, and possibly an interview or references.
  4. Wait for approvalProcessing can take weeks to a few months depending on the town. Plan ahead of the season you want to hunt.
  5. Buy and store legallyBuy from a licensed dealer, register the transaction as required, and follow MA storage and transport laws (locked or unloaded and cased).
Rules changed a lot in 2024 to 2026. Confirm current requirements with your police department and the MassWildlife firearms page.

Fishing

The easiest place to start: no course required.

  1. Buy a freshwater fishing licenseRequired at age 15 and up (free for ages 15 to 17 and 70 and older). Included in a Sporting license. Carry it while fishing.
  2. Add a saltwater permit for the coastFishing in salt water needs a separate Massachusetts recreational saltwater permit, also on MassFishHunt.
  3. Learn the basic gear rulesMax 2 hooks in open water, 5 when ice fishing. Lead sinkers and jigs under 1 ounce are banned in fresh water. Some fish must be released immediately.
  4. Check limits and special watersMost species are open all year, with daily limits and minimum lengths. Wachusett, Sudbury, and Quabbin reservoirs have their own seasons and shoreline rules.
  5. Find stocked waterMassWildlife stocks trout in spring and fall. Check the daily stocking report before you go.

Trapping

Separate course and license from hunting.

  1. Take Trapper EducationRequired for first-time trappers. MassWildlife runs a limited number of in-person courses, mostly late summer and fall.
  2. Buy a trapping licenseOn MassFishHunt after you're certified.
  3. Learn which traps are legalMassachusetts bans foothold and most body-gripping traps on land. Box and cage traps are the main legal option; the course covers the exceptions.
  4. Mark traps and follow check timesTraps must be marked with your identification and checked on a set schedule. Know the furbearer seasons before setting anything.

Archery

Start with a lesson where gear is provided, then add range time. Clubs are only needed later for outdoor and 3D practice.

Bowhunting rules in Massachusetts: bows need at least a 40 lb draw weight; broadheads must be steel and at least 7/8 inch wide; crossbows only with a crossbow permit.

Where: no shooting across or within 150 feet of a paved road, and no hunting within 500 feet of an occupied building without the owner's permission.

License: hunter ed, a Hunting or Sporting license, and the Archery Stamp. No separate bowhunter course is required.
